I am a fourth-year PhD candidate in Computer Science at Stony Brook University, where I am advised by Dr. Steven Skiena and work on machine translation and how we measure it. I expect to graduate in May 2028.

My work builds reference-free translation evaluation, most recently by turning iterative round-trip translation into training data, reaching state-of-the-art correlation with human judgments and outperforming xCOMET.

I am also interested in what happens to meaning under pressure: how far generation can be pushed when a model is forbidden a letter, a word, or a structure. That line of work produced an ‘e’-less translation of The Great Gatsby. Currently I am building a multilingual textbook translation and PDF-to-LaTeX pipeline that holds terminology and style consistent at book length.

Before the PhD I spent two years as a software engineer, building canvas-based graph visualization systems and AST tooling for early-stage startups.

selected publications

  1. EMNLP
    Evaluating Language Translation Models by Playing Telephone
    Syeda Jannatus Saba and Steven Skiena
    In Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP), 2025
    Oral presentation
    Abs Bib HTML

    Reference-based translation metrics require costly human translations. We introduce a synthetic data generation framework that trains translation-evaluation models on iterative round-trip ("telephone") translations, achieving state-of-the-art correlation with human judgments and surpassing xCOMET.

  2. IJCNLP-AACL
    Gatsby without the ’E’: Creating Lipograms with LLMs
    Rohan Balasubramanian, Nitish Gokulakrishnan, Syeda Jannatus Saba, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of IJCNLP-AACL 2025, 2025
    Oral presentation
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML

    We investigate constrained text generation with large language models by producing an ’e’-less translation of The Great Gatsby, analyzing how semantic fidelity degrades as letter restrictions tighten.
